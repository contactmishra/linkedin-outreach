# Messages

Language and style come from `data/profile.md` (style rules). Default: English, warm, short, specific, no buzzwords, no flattery overload, first name only, no sign-off (it's a chat).

## 1. Thank-you (on acceptance, no ask)
3 to 4 sentences, under about 400 characters.
1. Thank them for accepting.
2. ONE specific common point taken from their profile (shared tool, domain, place, school, career path, a post or project of theirs).
3. One line on the user's matching work, so the link is clear.
4. "Great to be connected!" or similar.

Common-point sources, best first: same niche or tools, same industry or domain, same city/region/country, same university, their own project, post or newsletter, their company's work. The user's material for this is in `profile.md`.

Example (user: SIEM engineer; contact: detection engineer who uses Python and AI):
> Hi Sam, thank you for accepting my request! Your work building detections with Python and AI is very close to mine. At my company I write SIEM detections and build LLM-assisted alert triage. Great to be connected!

## 2. CV message (`cvDelayDays` after the thank-you; needs the CV file)
Under about 700 characters, CV PDF attached:
1. Short opener (don't repeat the thank-you).
2. The user is exploring a move to <country/company> as <target role>; one line on what they bring.
3. If the company has a matching open role, name it exactly.
4. The ask, by group:
   - peer: "Would you be open to referring me, or pointing me to the person who handles hiring for your team?"
   - lead: "Are you hiring for a role like this in your team, or do you expect to be?"
   - recruiter: "Is there anything open that could fit my profile?"
5. "I've attached my CV. Thanks a lot for any help!"

## 3. Follow-up (`followUpDays` after the CV message, once)
1 to 2 sentences, a friendly nudge, no guilt:
> Hi <name>, just bringing this back up in case it got buried. Any pointer would mean a lot, and no worries if now isn't a good time!

## Replies
Flag them to the user. Claude may draft an answer; the user sends it.
